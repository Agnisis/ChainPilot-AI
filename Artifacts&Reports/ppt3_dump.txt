No Predictive Visibility

Discussion — Interpretation of Results


Why are MAPE values high?
The dataset contains only 100 records with high inter-SKU variance (σ > 320 units). With such diversity in product types (haircare, skincare, cosmetics), models have limited data to learn stable patterns. This is a known limitation of small, cross-sectional supply chain datasets.


What do the models successfully reveal?
Despite high MAPE, Random Forest and XGBoost correctly rank features by importance and identify non-linear relationships between variables. ARIMA successfully captures the sequential nature of SKU-level demand. Isolation Forest correctly flags 8% of records as anomalous — consistent with real-world defect rates in the dataset.


Model Comparison Insight
Random Forest (MAPE 56.7%) slightly outperforms XGBoost (57.9%) for cost prediction — indicating ensemble tree methods are well-suited for tabular supply chain data. ARIMA's high MAPE reflects the non-stationary, high-variance nature of SKU-level units sold, which are not a true time series.


Implications for Supply Chain Management
Even with prediction uncertainty, Isolation Forest's anomaly flagging provides immediate practical value — 8 records with abnormal patterns would warrant manual review. Feature importance from XGBoost shows shipping costs, manufacturing costs, and defect rates are top predictors of total costs.
